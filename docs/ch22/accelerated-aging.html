Why a 1000-hour test can stand in for years

Heat speeds up wear-out. Move the sliders and watch the wire wear out faster. (Electromigration is the star; TDDB, HCI and BTI are listed below.)

Electromigration in one wire

Arrhenius acceleration factor Ea illustrative

-acceleration factor AF
-years of use in a 1000 h HTOL
-animation speed

   
AF = exp[(Ea/k)(1/Tuse − 1/Tstress)], temperatures in kelvin, k = 8.617×10−5 eV/K. Ea depends on the failure mechanism and materials; 0.7 eV is only a placeholder. The model covers temperature only; voltage and humidity add their own terms.

  

  

   
FIT and MTTF values illustrative

   

   

   

   

   
1 FIT = 1 failure per 109 device-hours. MTTF = 109/FIT hours holds only if the failure rate is constant (the flat middle of the bathtub curve). With a constant rate, about 63% of devices have failed by the time MTTF is reached, so MTTF is not a guaranteed lifetime.

  

 






 
EM (electromigration)Long-term current pushes metal atoms; voids open the wire.

 
TDDBInsulating layer breaks down after long exposure to an electric field.

 
HCIHigh-energy electrons enter the gate insulator; the transistor changes slowly.

 
BTIHigh temperature plus voltage shifts the threshold voltage Vt.
