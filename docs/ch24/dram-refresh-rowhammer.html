Why DRAM needs refresh, and what Row Hammer does

A DRAM bit is charge in a tiny capacitor, and capacitors leak. Move the sliders and watch which cells lose their bit. All numbers are illustrative.

Model (illustrative): cell charge decays as e-t/τ with τ between 100 and 250 ms (halved when hot); a hammered victim loses an extra 0.5 × (count / 150,000) × (time / window); a bit is lost when charge falls below 50%. Real thresholds, retention times and mitigations vary by chip and generation. Typical refresh window 64 ms (32 ms when hot) and 8192 refresh commands per window are common DDR3/DDR4 values; 350 ns per command is an example.
